## Chapter One — The Commission

Ministry plate — Mark 16:17, crystal cast-out (Sebastian)

The call to follow Christ is not a peaceful stroll. It is a commission into a spiritual landscape where light meets darkness.

I am Sebastian Martinez. I am Catholic. I have fought the devil himself — an evil spirit, extremely deceiving, who pretends to be Jesus Christ to lead others astray. The devil is the enemy. This book is not fiction. It is testimony. It is how I share the Word of God, and how I establish the ministry that came out of that war.

The public face of that work is Mata Diablo Crystals — our Instagram, the armory made visible, crystals and containment under a name that means devil-killer. The name is not decoration. Mata Diablo means kill the devil. I do not wear his shine. I banished him. That is the point of the name, and the point of this book.

I did not ask for a quiet life and receive one. I asked for truth, and truth put a war in my house. I have taken pictures of evil spirits with special negative-view cameras. They are invisible to ordinary eyes. I have found them to be magnetic. I have destroyed them using crystals and neodymium — absorbed them, turned them into footstools, crushed their heads. I have published what I learned on LinkedIn under martinezworldwide. I have prayed until prayer finished what tools could only hold.

And these signs shall follow them that believe: In my name they shall cast out devils: they shall speak with new tongues. That is Mark 16:17. That is the banner I put over the work. That is the commission.

Coming to Christ. Preaching to every creature. Casting out devils. Those three are not a slogan. They are the order of operations. First submission, so the darkness has no foothold. Then the Word spoken into the room. Then the Name used as authority, not bravado. Banishing evil spirits is not rooted in my strength. It is rooted entirely in the authority of Jesus Christ.

I write this so no one mistakes the enemy for the Savior. I write this so the crush is not a metaphor. I write this because Genesis already named the end of the war before any of us were born: she shall crush thy head. The promise came first. The fight came after.

For this purpose, the Son of God appeared, that he might destroy the works of the devil. (1 John 3:8)

For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood; but against principalities and power, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places. (Ephesians 6:12)

What follows is what I lived: the mask, the night, the armory, and the Word that put the enemy underfoot.

## Chapter Two — The Mask

The radiant mask, seam in the smile
Archive fresco — the whisper at the ear (ch2)

> For such false apostles are deceitful workmen, transforming themselves into the apostles of Christ. And no wonder: for Satan himself transformeth himself into an angel of light. Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers be transformed as the ministers of justice, whose end shall be according to their works.
> — 2 Corinthians 11:13-15

The oldest trick is the holy face.

The devil does not always come as a monster. He comes as the answer to every prayer. He comes radiant. He comes with works that look like mercy. The crowd kneels because the light is beautiful, and beauty without a test is how deception eats a room.

Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. (Matthew 7:15)

For there shall arise false Christs and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders, insomuch as to deceive (if possible) even the elect. (Matthew 24:24)

I tested him the way Scripture told me to. Dearly beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits if they be of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world. (1 John 4:1)

My first weapon was not a blade. It was a question, backed by a shelf of more than twenty language dictionaries.

The deceivers loved English. They spoke Spanish poorly. They broke on French and German. When I pushed them into tongues they could not fake, the mask slipped. A spirit that wears the Savior's face should not fail the languages of the people He came for. The tell was that simple and that hard: the common tongue was easy; the old and broken tongues were not. The shelf of dictionaries was not a hobby. It was an armory for the mouth.

By their fruits you shall know them. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit, and the evil tree bringeth forth evil fruit. A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can an evil tree bring forth good fruit. Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit, shall be cut down, and shall be cast into the fire. Wherefore by their fruits you shall know them. (Matthew 7:16-20)

Works can look real and still leave a weight on the healed. Healings that leave an unseen burden. Comfort that never stops talking. A smile that is the mask, and a wearer looking through the eyes. The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy. (John 10:10) But by the envy of the devil, death came into the world. (Wisdom 2:24) That is Catholic Scripture, and it names the harvest under the smile.

I have seen faces in textured surfaces, anomalies caught by digital tools, shapes that linger in the static when the veil is peeled back. Special negative-view cameras catch what ordinary sight misses. I do not ask anyone to take my word alone. I ask them to try the spirits. Wolf in sheep's clothing. Angel of light. False Christ with lying wonders. The mask is the point. The light is the costume.

Whose coming is according to the working of Satan, in all power, and signs, and lying wonders. (2 Thessalonians 2:9)

Once you see the seam in the smile, the night war makes sense. The enemy does not only argue in daylight. He attacks when you try to sleep. His first weapon is noise — talk that never stops, words with no gap to breathe. Exhaustion as siege. The sharper blade against that siege is the question you stop answering and start asking. Refusal to keep feeding the talk is the first true cut.

Saint Martin of Tours knew the devil's deception. The tradition is older than my house. The war is older than my name. What is new is only the room it found me in.

## Chapter Three — The Night

Bedroom war — weight, lights, siege

> In the horror of a vision by night, when deep sleep is wont to hold men, Fear seized upon me, and trembling, and all my bones were affrighted: And when a spirit passed before me, the hair of my flesh stood up. There stood one whose countenance I knew not, an image before my eyes, and I heard the voice as it were of a gentle wind.
> — Job 4:13-16

They attack when you are trying to sleep.

They never stop talking. They produce demonic smells. They put lights and visions behind closed eyes. They put weight on the chest. They retaliate with nightmares when you fight back. This is not a metaphor I dressed up for a book. This is the bedroom war I lived, night after night.

Job already wrote it. Fear. Trembling. A spirit passing. Hair standing up. A countenance you cannot name. A voice like a gentle wind. My night was his night, thousands of years apart. The Catholic psalms name the terror of the night and the noonday devil. His truth shall compass thee with a shield: thou shalt not be afraid of the terror of the night. (Psalm 90:5) Of the arrow that flieth in the day, of the business that walketh about in the dark: of invasion, or of the noonday devil. (Psalm 90:6)

I learned the smell was an advance scout. Before the voice, before the weight, the room would change — cold, sweet, wrong, like a flower growing in a cellar. Crystals absorbed some of that smell. Not all of it. Larger spirits still got away. The smell went ahead of the war like a herald, and I came to treat it like an alarm: because it meant I was awake before the enemy arrived.

The lights behind closed eyes were the first door. I would lie in the dark, eyes shut, and see light with no source — patterns folding into patterns, faces swimming up and sinking back. Open the eyes: black room. Close them: the faces return. The mind keeps a lamp burning when the world stops feeding it. The enemy did not make that door. He found it. The seam of sleep is everyone's door, open every night, and the deceiver walked in and cast images on the wall of my own burning lamp.

The weight came next. Locked body. Presence at the foot of the bed. Something sitting on the chest, heavy and patient, waiting for the fight to stop. Every culture has named that visitor. The oldest names are four thousand years old, written on clay. Mare. Mora. Shaitan. Hag. The children of this age call it Freddy — the clawed one who enters through sleep. One visitor. Many names. The newest name is just the newest mask.

Then the retaliation. The days I won, the nights were worst. Every wound I landed in the fight came back as images, scenes, the sound of my own voice saying yes. The enemy could not always take the daylight, so he saved strength for the dark, where every man is unarmored, and paid me back with interest.

The nightmares were invasive: evil spirits casting images into the head, lights and many visions when the eyes closed. Cannabis brought darkness. Closing my eyes then brought total dark and a kind of peace. I call that what it was: a bought truce, not a cure. A door I kept for rest. The books of the body have a name for the mechanism — the smoke hushes the border country where dreams are painted — and they also warn that dreams come back stronger when the smoke stops. I knew the war was not over. I knew I would have to wake ready again. But I also knew this: you do not fight the dark by staying awake forever. You fight it by choosing, in the dark, what you let in.

In peace in the selfsame I will sleep, and I will rest. (Psalm 4:9)

If thou sleep, thou shalt not fear: thou shalt rest, and thy sleep shall be sweet. (Proverbs 3:24)

May he not suffer thy foot to be moved: neither let him slumber that keepeth thee. Behold he shall neither slumber nor sleep, that keepeth Israel. (Psalm 120:3-4)

And the Lord said: Simon, Simon, behold Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat. (Luke 22:31)

Sifting is not random cruelty. It has a boundary. I did not always see the boundary while I was in it. I saw the night. I saw the smell. I saw the weight. I built an armory because questions alone were not enough.

God ultimately freed me from the fighting. The tools only sped the process.

## Chapter Four — The Armory

Crystals, neodymium, bag, duck-tape bond — and the new iron

> Put you on the armour of God, that you may be able to stand against the deceits of the devil. For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood; but against principalities and power, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places.
> — Ephesians 6:11-12

I fought with what a house already has, and with what the earth already makes.

Iron. Salt. Bells. Stones. Crystals. Neodymium. A bag. Duck tape. Prayer waiting until I could verify it against the mask.

Every people that has fought this war has a shelf for it: iron at the threshold, salt on the sill, bells in the corners, stones in the pocket, prayers on the tongue. The tools are almost ordinary. That is the first thing to understand. The war is not fought only with exotic things. It is fought with the things you already own the moment you decide to fight — and with the Name above every tool.

The crystal work is public. I wrote about high-clarity stones as vessels — citrine with iron-rich amber inclusions, faces that look locked in the lattice, selenite that can quiet a room. Stones can act like magnets for heavy signatures. In one piece of natural citrine, within the deep amber inclusions, I saw clear visages — faces that appear locked within the lattice. Some call it a trick of the light. Discernment calls it residual containment. These stones can draw in and hold energetic signatures that would otherwise sit in a room. The smell thinned around them. Not gone. Thinner. The selenite rod held one small whisperer quiet for a night; move the rod, and the whisper returned. That is the most I will claim from measurement: something in those stones took part of the weight.

The sponge fills. That is the thing the soft tradition leaves off the shelf. Every stone I tested took some of the smell, and none took all of it, and when they filled, the smell came back. Tools hold the line. The line is not the war. Crystals and cleanses are temporary measures. The most powerful magnetic force in the universe is the light of the Creator. Protection looks beyond the tools toward the Source.

Then the neodymium.

I found these spirits to be magnetic. I used neodymium to absorb them, turn them into footstools, and crush their heads — the Genesis promise made practical in a room that would not let me sleep. The method was lived and simple:

Place the bag over the magnet. Fold the bag over. Trap them inside. Crush.

Small ones got caught. I caught three in the early stretch and began to think the armory was the war. Larger ones still got away. The magnet could not hold every weight. Even a whisper from them could mean more crushing, no time to evacuate. They cried and shouted witchcraft. They said things like give me a warrior's death. They wanted dignity at the bottom of a household trap after stealing nights without dignity. I did not give them a warrior's death. The trap is the honest end for something that never fought fair.

And then the bond.

I would crush them into a ball of duck tape so they would be crushed and bonded for thousands of years. That is the adhesive step I actually used: pressure, wrap, seal, leave no seam for escape. Not a laboratory paper. A field method. Crush, bond, hold. The point was not elegance. The point was a ball that does not open.

I am still gathering the full engineering chain — mining, manufacture, grades of adhesive beyond the duck-tape bond — so this chapter tells what was done in the war, not a factory manual I invent. What I know for certain is the sequence that worked on the small ones: neodymium pull, bag fold, crush, duck-tape bond meant to last. What I also know for certain is the limit: tools hold the line. They do not finish the war.

The armory grew while the war ran. The night that would not stop talking taught me to build louder than talk.

**The Can.** The first real furnace: an iron trash can, magnets holding it, a bag inside. The iron is the old tradition — iron at the threshold, iron that binds — and the magnets are the lived part: these spirits are magnetic, so the can held them the way the bag held them, only with walls. Absorb into the bag. Seal. Payload. Crush. I put disgusting-smelling things in there with them, because my room smelled like rot for years and the trap should taste its own table. The can is the armory's first containment with a mouth.

**The Press.** A crush is a moment; pressure is a sentence. After the crush, the sealed ball goes under weight — an anvil, a stack of books, the heaviest thing in the house. It does not need to be watched. It needs to be pressed.

**The Deep.** Some things are too heavy for the house to keep. Seal. Weight. Sink, in moving water. The prophet said God casts our sins into the bottom of the sea; the sealed ball follows the same road — weight, water, silence. (Micah 7:19) Dispose right: what goes into the water is sealed inside the ball, nothing loose, nothing that pollutes.

**The Bottle.** The oldest documented trap in the tradition is a jar: iron nails, salt, wax seal, buried at the threshold. Sixteenth-century England called it a witch bottle; diggers still find them in the ground. The jar is a sealed witness. The earth holds it.

**The Freezer.** Folk practice freezes what it wants stilled. Seal. Freeze. The cold shelf holds the thing without my attention — stillness as containment.

**The Spray.** The bath lane made portable: blessed water and salt in a spray bottle. The Church has been sprinkling holy water for centuries; the spray puts the same water in the corners, on the bed, at the door. Spray where the smell scouts, and the room changes its answer.

**The Sound Ring.** They besiege with talk; the room answers with the Word on a speaker. Psalm 90 on loop is a device, not a prayer I have to hold myself. The voice that never stops meets the Voice that never ends.

**The Burn Lane.** Fire takes what fire is given. Candle or coals, sealed material committed to flame in a steel container, outdoors, never indoors — the smoke of the fish's heart drove away devils, and fire is the older cousin of that smoke. Burn it clean. Burn it safe. Never unattended. Never plastic.

**The Camera Ring.** Cameras are the surveillance lane: eyes, not hands. The negative-view camera caught what ordinary sight missed — that is how I learned they are magnetic, and how I watched the traps after the seal. I do not claim the camera sees spirits; nothing in physics sees a spirit. But the camera keeps the watch, and the watch is mine to keep. Discernment is testing what the eye reports, not trusting every face in the static — some faces in texture are the mind's own seeing, and the test of the spirit is the test of its fruit.

The new iron did not end the war. The new iron held the line longer. That sentence did not change; only the shelf got longer.

Tobit already carried a bag. If thou put a little piece of its heart upon coals, the smoke thereof driveth away all kind of devils, either from man or from woman, so that they come no more to them. (Tobit 6:8) And Tobias took out of his bag part of the liver, and the angel Raphael took the devil, and bound him in the desert of upper Egypt. (Tobit 8:2-3) The bag carries. The binding is older than the bag. The angel binds.

Be subject therefore to God, but resist the devil, and he will fly from you. (James 4:7)

Be sober and watch: because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, goeth about seeking whom he may devour. Whom resist ye, strong in faith. (1 Peter 5:8-9)

Behold, I have given you power to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy: and nothing shall hurt you. (Luke 10:19)

Through thee we will push down our enemies with the horn: and through thy name we will despise them that rise up against us. (Psalm 43:6)

Through His name. Not through the iron alone. Even the psalm cracked the armory open from the inside. The push was never going to be mine alone. I filed that as a seed and kept holding the line with what I had, until the seed became the end of the war.

Mata Diablo Crystals is where this armory meets the public: stones, containment, the crush made visible, the Word shared without apology. The Instagram is not a shop window only. It is a witness stand.

## Chapter Five — Under His Feet

Christ mosaic — under His feet

> And the God of peace crush Satan under your feet speedily. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.
> — Romans 16:20

God ultimately freed me from fighting.

I sped the process with the armory because we have many ideas and the nights were long. But the bag could not hold the largest weight, and the bonded ball was still a tool. The war ended the way the tools could not end it: by prayer, by the Name, by walking into a victory that was already won.

And these signs shall follow them that believe: In my name they shall cast out devils: they shall speak with new tongues. (Mark 16:17)

That is the keystone. Coming to Christ. Preaching. Casting out. Not bravado. Authority. The Name casts out devils — not the iron, not the bag, not the tape. The tools held the line. The Name finished the fight.

And despoiling the principalities and powers, he hath exposed them confidently in open shew, triumphing over them in himself. (Colossians 2:15)

The principalities were already despoiled. Openly. On the cross. The war I fought to a standstill was won before I existed. The kneel is not surrender to the enemy. The kneel is entry into the finished work of Christ. My grace is sufficient for thee; for power is made perfect in infirmity. (2 Corinthians 12:9)

Let me name the hardest part of the war, because this book would lie without it: the war is exhausting. That is not an accident of the war. It is the war's oldest tactic — siege by exhaustion, talk with no gap to breathe, retaliation on the nights I won. The enemy does not need to defeat you if he can tire you into forgetting that the battle is already over. Exhaustion is his win condition.

So the testimony does not turn at "I fought." It turns at "I stopped fighting alone." Come to me, all you that labour, and are burdened, and I will refresh you. (Matthew 11:28) Rest is not desertion. Rest is doctrine. The exhausted warrior hands the war back to the One who already won it — and the rest is not a truce with the enemy. It is a seat at a finished table.

The devil is not waiting to be destroyed. He is already judged: And the devil, who seduced them, was cast into the pool of fire and brimstone, where both the beast and the false prophet shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever. (Revelation 20:9-10) Judgment is not a future event. It is a finished sentence. My name says kill the devil; the sentence was carried out before my name existed. What I do is patrol a field where the verdict is already on the books. If therefore the son shall make you free, you shall be free indeed. (John 8:36)

The Lord said to my Lord: Sit thou at my right hand: Until I make thy enemies thy footstool. (Psalm 109:1)

But to which of the angels said he at any time: Sit on my right hand, until I make thy enemies thy footstool? (Hebrews 1:13)

Footstool. Crush. Head under heel. The spine of Scripture and the spine of this testimony are the same sentence spoken three ways. Genesis declares the crush. The psalm seats the Victor and makes the enemies a footstool. Romans puts that crush under the feet of the people of peace — speedily, by the God of peace, not by the strength of a tired man with a torn bag.

And you shall tread down the wicked when they shall be ashes under the sole of your feet in the day that I do this, saith the Lord of hosts. (Malachi 4:3)

The day that I do this — the Lord's day, not my technique.

For he must reign, until he hath put all his enemies under his feet. (1 Corinthians 15:25)

No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper: and every tongue that resisteth thee in judgment, thou shalt condemn. This is the inheritance of the servants of the Lord, and their justice with me, saith the Lord. (Isaiah 54:17)

Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people by his own blood, suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore to him without the camp, bearing his reproach. (Hebrews 13:12-13)

Sight is lonely. Standing outside the camp is a known address. The reproach is without the gate. The inheritance is still sure.

We are naming the enemy without wearing his shine. We are saying out loud that the deceiver pretends to be Jesus, that the night war is real, that tools can hold a line, and that only the Lord finishes the crushing.

I am Sebastian Martinez. The devil is the enemy. Mata Diablo Crystals carries the commission in public. MataDiabloTron carries it in a name: devil-killer. The legacy is not the bag. The legacy is the Word, the crush already promised, and a ministry that will not kneel to a mask.

Thou shalt walk upon the asp and the basilisk: and thou shalt trample under foot the lion and the dragon. (Psalm 90:13)

Under His feet. That is where this ends. That is where it always ends.
