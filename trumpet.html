The Epstein Deal: how a U.S. attorney buried a federal case, and what came of it

Feed post: The Trumpet No. 2 (2026-08-30). Spine: Proverbs 28:4.

  1. 2005 — Police in Palm Beach, Florida begin investigating Jeffrey Epstein after a parent reports he paid her 14-year-old stepdaughter to strip and massage him.
  2. Investigation — Investigators identify 36 girls, ages 14 to 17, with similar accounts of sexual abuse.
  3. Federal case — In the Southern District of Florida, federal prosecutor Marie Villafaña drafts a 53-page, 60-count indictment on enticement and trafficking of minors. U.S. Attorney Alexander Acosta is not ready to go forward with the indictment.
  4. September 2007 — Instead of the indictment: a non-prosecution agreement (NPA). Epstein receives immunity from all federal criminal charges, extended to four named co-conspirators and any unnamed "potential co-conspirators." The agreement is kept secret from the victims.
  5. 2008 — Epstein pleads guilty to state charges (procuring a minor for prostitution, soliciting a prostitute), is registered as a sex offender, and serves 13 months in jail with work release. Per the Miami Herald, the NPA "essentially shut down an ongoing FBI probe into whether there were more victims and other powerful people who took part in Epstein's sex crimes."
  6. 2018 — The Miami Herald publishes its investigation (Julie K. Brown), reopening public scrutiny of the plea deal and its victims.
  7. February 2019 — U.S. District Judge Kenneth Marra (S.D. Fla.) rules the NPA violated the Crime Victims' Rights Act.
  8. July 2019 — The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York (Geoffrey Berman) indicts Epstein on sex trafficking of minors and conspiracy (2002–2005). Acosta resigns as Secretary of Labor under bipartisan criticism.
  9. August 2019 — Epstein dies in federal custody; his death is ruled a suicide.
  10. 2020 — The 11th Circuit Court of Appeals reverses Judge Marra's ruling: the prosecutors did not violate the Crime Victims' Rights Act. The deal stands.
  11. December 2021 — Ghislaine Maxwell is convicted of sex trafficking girls to Epstein.
  12. Settlements — Epstein's estate (~$600M) has paid hundreds of millions to at least 200 women; JPMorgan settled for $290M and Deutsche Bank for $75M over enabling the abuse.
  13. March 2025 — Alexander Acosta joins the board of directors of Newsmax, where he chairs the audit committee.
